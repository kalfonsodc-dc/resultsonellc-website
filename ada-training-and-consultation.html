ADA Training and Consultation

The Americans with Disabilities Act applies to far more than ramps and parking spaces. It reaches hiring and accommodation decisions, the events you host, the documents you publish, and the websites and applications people use to reach your services. Results One helps organizations understand what the law actually requires of them, and build the practices that meet it.

We work with public entities covered by Title II and with businesses open to the public under Title III. Our consultants include certified accessibility professionals and testers who use assistive technology daily, so the guidance you get reflects how your services are experienced, not only how they are documented.

What we provide

  • ADA compliance consulting: A clear reading of which titles and obligations apply to your organization, where you currently stand, and what to prioritize first.
  • Staff and leadership training: Practical sessions on ADA requirements, the interactive accommodation process, and the decisions managers are most often asked to make.
  • Accommodation process support: Help designing and documenting a request process your staff can follow consistently and your employees can trust.
  • Policy and practice review: Assessment of HR policies, hiring practices, event planning, and service delivery against ADA requirements.
  • Accessibility planning: A sequenced plan with owners and timelines, covering physical access, communication access, and digital access together.
  • Communication access: Guidance on effective communication obligations, including interpreters, captioning, and alternative formats.
  • Ongoing advisory support: Access to our team as questions come up, so decisions get made with the requirements in view.

Title II: state and local government

In April 2024 the Department of Justice published its final rule under Title II, requiring state and local governments to bring their websites, mobile applications, and digital content into conformance with WCAG 2.1 Level AA. An Interim Final Rule issued on April 20, 2026 extended the deadlines: jurisdictions serving 50,000 or more residents now have until April 26, 2027, and smaller jurisdictions and special district governments until April 26, 2028.

The extension is time to do the work, not a reason to wait. Conformance takes manual testing, remediation, validation with disabled users, staff training, policy development, and ongoing monitoring. For most organizations that is a programme measured in months.

Title III: businesses open to the public

Though the ADA predates the web, courts and the DOJ have consistently read Title III as reaching the websites and mobile apps of businesses serving the public. If customers book, buy, apply, or get information from you online, that channel is part of what the law covers, and it is where a growing share of complaints and lawsuits originate.

How we work

We start with where you are rather than a generic checklist: what you are responsible for, what you have already done, and what carries the most risk and the most benefit to the people you serve. Training is built around your actual policies and scenarios, delivered in person or on remote platforms, and sized to the audience, whether that is a leadership briefing or a working session with the staff who handle requests day to day.

Compliance is the floor. The organizations we work with generally want the same thing underneath it: for disabled employees and members of the public to be able to do what everyone else can do, without having to ask for an exception.
