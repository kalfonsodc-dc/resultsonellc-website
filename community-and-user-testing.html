Community & User Accessibility Testing

Digital Accessibility Services › Community & User Accessibility Testing

Accessibility Testing Informed by Expertise—and Lived Experience

Automated tools can identify accessibility errors. Technical testing can determine whether digital content meets specific accessibility criteria. But neither, by itself, can fully answer the most important question:

Can a person with a disability actually use it?

Results One LLC provides Community & User Accessibility Testing that brings together professional accessibility expertise, assistive technology testing, and the lived experiences of people with disabilities.

Our accessibility testing team includes professionals who are IAAP certified and DHS Trusted Testers, with more than 10 years of accessibility testing experience. Our certified testing team also includes professionals who are blind or visually impaired and use assistive technology in their everyday lives.

That combination matters.

Our testers don’t simply simulate how someone might use a screen reader or encounter an accessibility barrier. They bring professional testing methodology together with firsthand knowledge of how inaccessible digital experiences affect independence, efficiency, privacy, and equal access.

Beyond Automated Testing

Automated accessibility testing is valuable, but it can identify only certain types of accessibility issues. A website can pass an automated check and still present significant barriers to a person trying to complete a real-world task.

That is why Results One incorporates manual testing, keyboard testing, assistive technology testing, and user-centered evaluation as appropriate to the engagement. Our testing may include:

  • Screen reader testing
  • Keyboard-only navigation
  • Screen magnification
  • Forms and interactive controls
  • Navigation and menu functionality
  • Accessible names and labels
  • Headings and page structure
  • Alternative text and image context
  • Error identification and recovery
  • Modal dialogs and dynamic content
  • Documents and downloadable content
  • Multimedia accessibility
  • Complex transactions and user journeys

Assistive technologies may include JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver, screen magnification, and other technologies appropriate to the testing environment and user population.

Testing Real Tasks, Not Just Individual Elements

Accessibility is ultimately about whether someone can accomplish what they came to your digital property to do. Results One can design testing around real-world user journeys rather than evaluating individual components in isolation.

For example, we may ask testers to:

  • Locate important information
  • Navigate a website independently
  • Register for a program or service
  • Complete and submit a form
  • Find and read a PDF or document
  • Search for information
  • Create or access an account
  • Complete a transaction
  • Navigate an application using assistive technology
  • Perform other mission-critical tasks specific to your organization

This approach can reveal barriers that may not be obvious when individual pages or components are evaluated separately.

Technical Compliance + Real-World Usability

There is an important difference between technical conformance and a usable experience. A digital component may technically satisfy a particular accessibility requirement while still being confusing, inefficient, or difficult for someone using assistive technology.

Our testing helps organizations understand both. Where appropriate, findings can identify the applicable WCAG success criterion, severity, location, affected users, accessibility impact, testing method, and recommended remediation. User-testing observations can provide additional context about what happened when a person using assistive technology attempted the task.

Instead of simply telling your development team that something failed, we help explain:

What happened. Who it affects. Why it matters. And what should be done about it.

Include People With Disabilities in the Process

Too often, organizations make accessibility decisions for people with disabilities without meaningfully including them in the evaluation process. Results One helps change that.

Depending on the engagement, we can incorporate people with disabilities and assistive technology users into structured testing, focus groups, feedback sessions, and usability evaluations. Participants can provide insight into questions such as:

  • Can I find what I need?
  • Can I understand the information?
  • Can I complete the task independently?
  • Can I use the experience efficiently with my assistive technology?
  • Does the design create unnecessary barriers even when it technically conforms?

These perspectives can provide organizations with insights that automated reports alone cannot deliver.

Lived Experience Is Part of Our Expertise

At Results One, lived experience does not replace technical accessibility testing. It strengthens it.

Our approach combines trained accessibility professionals, established testing methodologies, assistive technology, recognized accessibility standards, and firsthand experience navigating digital environments with disabilities.

For organizations selecting an accessibility partner, this provides something important: findings informed not only by what accessibility standards require, but also by an understanding of why those requirements matter to the people they are intended to serve.

Who Can Benefit?

Community and User Accessibility Testing can support:

  • Federal, state, and local government agencies
  • Nonprofit organizations
  • Corporations
  • Educational institutions
  • Healthcare organizations
  • Technology companies
  • Website and application developers
  • Government contractors
  • Organizations preparing for digital accessibility compliance initiatives
  • Product teams developing or redesigning digital experiences

Testing can be incorporated into an initial accessibility assessment, performed before a website or application launch, used during development, or conducted after remediation to evaluate the improved user experience.
