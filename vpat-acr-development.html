VPAT/ACR Development

Digital Accessibility Services › VPAT/ACR Development

Organizations purchasing technology increasingly want to understand whether digital products and services meet accessibility requirements before making procurement decisions. Results One LLC provides VPAT and Accessibility Conformance Report development services to help technology providers clearly document the accessibility of their products.

The Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) is a standardized template used to document how a technology product conforms to applicable accessibility standards. Once completed, the resulting document is generally referred to as an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR).

VPAT/ACR development can be especially valuable for technology companies and vendors seeking to do business with federal, state, and local governments, educational institutions, corporations, and organizations with established accessibility procurement requirements.

Know—and Document—How Accessible Your Product Really Is

A VPAT should be more than a completed questionnaire. Results One LLC develops evidence-based Accessibility Conformance Reports (ACRs) based on accessibility testing of your product or service.

We evaluate the product against the applicable accessibility requirements and use the testing results to determine and document conformance. This gives prospective customers, procurement teams, and contracting officers meaningful information they can use when evaluating your product.

Which VPAT Editions Do We Support?

Results One can prepare ACRs using the applicable Information Technology Industry Council (ITI) VPAT® template based on your market and procurement requirements. Depending on the engagement, this may include:

  • WCAG Edition — For products evaluated against applicable Web Content Accessibility Guidelines criteria.
  • Revised Section 508 Edition — For organizations selling information and communication technology to the U.S. Federal Government or responding to procurements requiring Section 508 conformance information.
  • EN 301 549 Edition — For products requiring documentation against European accessibility requirements, including procurements where EN 301 549 is specified.
  • International (INT) Edition — For organizations that need an ACR addressing multiple applicable standards within a single template.

We use the appropriate current ITI VPAT® template for the engagement and confirm the applicable edition and accessibility standard before testing begins.

How Do We Determine Conformance?

We don’t determine conformance by simply asking your team whether the product is accessible. Our ACRs are grounded in testing. Depending on the product, our evaluation may combine:

  • Automated accessibility testing
  • Expert manual testing
  • Keyboard-only testing
  • Screen reader testing
  • Assistive technology testing
  • Review of product functionality and user workflows
  • Review of relevant supporting documentation

Testing may include assistive technologies such as JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver, screen magnification, and other appropriate technologies and testing environments.

Conformance terms such as Supports, Partially Supports, and Does Not Support are assigned based on the evidence gathered during the evaluation and the requirements of the applicable VPAT® template—not simply on vendor self-attestation.

What Do We Need From You?

Before testing begins, Results One works with your technical team to establish the testing environment and scope. Depending on the product, we may request:

  • Product or Build Access — Access to the production product or a stable testing build.
  • Test Credentials — User accounts and appropriate permissions necessary to evaluate applicable features and workflows.
  • Product Documentation — User guides, technical documentation, accessibility documentation, and other materials relevant to the evaluation.
  • Defined Scope — Identification of the product, version, platforms, features, and workflows included in the assessment.
  • Technical Contact — A designated member of your technical or product team who can answer questions and address access or functionality issues during testing.

Having these elements available at the beginning of the engagement helps minimize delays and allows our testers to focus on evaluating the product.

What Happens If We Find Accessibility Failures?

Finding accessibility issues does not prevent an ACR from being developed. In fact, an ACR should accurately communicate known accessibility limitations.

When testing identifies a criterion that is not fully supported, Results One documents the conformance level and provides appropriate explanatory remarks based on the testing evidence. We do not soften findings simply to make an ACR appear more favorable.

This transparency is important because procurement professionals rely on ACRs to understand potential accessibility barriers and evaluate whether a product meets their organization’s requirements.

If accessibility issues are identified, Results One can also discuss a separate remediation engagement with your organization. Remediation is distinct from the independent evaluation and ACR development process, helping preserve the integrity of the conformance reporting.

Following remediation, the affected functionality can be retested and the ACR can be updated when appropriate.

How Long Does VPAT/ACR Development Take?

The timeline depends on the size and complexity of the product, number of platforms, applicable standards, testing environments, workflows, and overall scope.

A relatively straightforward digital product may require a shorter evaluation period, while a complex enterprise application with multiple user roles, platforms, integrations, and workflows will require additional testing.

Results One establishes the anticipated timeline after an initial product and scope review.

Your team’s readiness also matters. Providing stable product access, credentials, documentation, and a responsive technical contact can significantly reduce delays.

How Is VPAT/ACR Development Priced?

VPAT/ACR engagements are scoped based on the product, testing complexity, applicable VPAT® edition, platforms, functionality, and number of workflows being evaluated. Pricing considerations may include:

  • Number of products or product versions
  • VPAT® edition required
  • Number of platforms or environments
  • Complexity of the product
  • Number of user roles and workflows
  • Testing requirements
  • Assistive technology testing requirements
  • Documentation requirements
  • Retesting requirements

If multiple VPAT® editions are required for the same product, we can identify that requirement during scoping and structure the engagement accordingly.

The proposal will also specify whether retesting and subsequent ACR updates are included or priced separately, so your organization understands the complete scope before work begins.

An ACR Procurement Teams Can Trust

A VPAT® is the template. The completed document—the Accessibility Conformance Report—is the evidence your customers and procurement teams evaluate.

Results One approaches ACR development as an accessibility evaluation, not a paperwork exercise.

Test. Document. Explain.

Our goal is to provide an ACR that accurately represents the accessibility of your product, clearly communicates known limitations, and gives procurement professionals useful information for making informed decisions.
